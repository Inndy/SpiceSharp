## Findings

- **L1: a guest whose display has powered down shows nothing, and the viewer gives no hint.** GNOME blanked the
  screen; spice-server then has no primary surface and sends none on connect. The viewer stayed at "Connecting to …"
  (all channels linked) for 90 s; a click and a Shift press in that window didn't bring up a picture within 3 s. Keys
  sent through spicerec's inputs channel did wake the guest. Whether the viewer forwards input before the first surface
  wasn't checked (its log wasn't found in the time left). spicerec's `--script` waits for the surface, so it can't wake
  such a guest either; the README now says to wake the guest first. Needs a decision: e.g. forward keyboard input
  before the first surface, and say "connected; the guest display is off, press a key" instead of "Connecting".
  **Fixed in code, live re-check pending** ([decision 0035](../decisions/0035-guest-display-off-takes-keys-and-the-monitor-area-is-shown.md)):
  the session reports the display as off after DISPLAY_MARK without a primary surface (or when the primary surface goes
  without a new one), the window says "Connected. The guest's display is off — press a key (Shift) to wake it.", and
  keys reach the inputs channel from link on; clicks still wait for a picture. Covered by `DisplayOffTests` (a replay
  of this guest's channels with the display channel cut to SET_ACK and MARK); the window itself and the wake on a
  live guest are not checked yet. **Re-check (below): partly fixed.** Keys wake the guest from "Connecting" and from
  the display-off state, pointer input is held back, the hook and presenter come back on wake. The message is never
  seen: a guest blanking while connected leaves the last frame on screen (L5), and a guest already blank at connect
  sends no DISPLAY_MARK, so the window stays at "Connecting to …" (L7).
- **L2: the canvas ignores `DISPLAY_MONITORS_CONFIG`, so a Linux guest shows a black band.** At 640x480 the qxl
  driver's primary surface is 640x512; spice-server reports the 640x480 monitor in `MONITORS_CONFIG`, and other clients
  show only that area. `Canvas` treats the message as a no-op (`Canvas.cs`, `DisplayMsg.MonitorsConfig`), so the
  viewer and the replay show the full surface with 32 black rows at the bottom (visible in the candidate golden). The
  window also follows the surface size, not the monitor size. Not seen on the Windows guests, whose surfaces match
  their modes. **Fixed in code, live re-check pending**
  ([decision 0035](../decisions/0035-guest-display-off-takes-keys-and-the-monitor-area-is-shown.md)): the canvas
  applies the first head on surface 0, and the presenter, window sizing, mouse mapping, resize-follows-window and
  snapshots use that 640x480 area; the replay of `ubuntu-rich` now gives a 640x480 image with no black band
  (`MonitorAreaTests`), whose new golden candidate awaits approval. The Windows fixtures' monitors equal their
  surfaces, so their goldens are unchanged. **Re-check (below): pass after a connect and after mode changes; open
  after a display wake (L6).** The padding rows come back, now holding stale guest memory, whenever the guest wakes
  its display: no DISPLAY_MONITORS_CONFIG follows that surface creation.
- **L3: the Linux guest sends no QUIC.** Mutter on the qxl KMS driver renders every update as a full-frame DrawCopy
  that spice-server compresses with GLZ (LZ for the first). The milestone 2 DoD asks for "a Linux guest recording,
  covering QUIC, LZ and GLZ"; this guest covers LZ and GLZ only. QUIC needs a guest or workload that produces
  photographic image draws (or a server set to `image-compression=quic`, a VM config change that needs the human's
  approval).
- **L4: the guest's display mode changed from 1024x768 to 640x480.** The greeter ran at 1024x768 (20:09); the
  logged-in desktop recorded at 20:13 ran at 640x480. The cause wasn't determined: either the user session's own
  monitor setting, or the 6 s viewer session at 20:10 asking for its window size through the agent
  (resize-follows-window; the window's client area was about 640x480 then). The guest may still be at 640x480.

### Findings

- **L5: the "display off" message is never visible over a shown display; the last frame stays.** When the guest
  blanked while connected, the log shows the presenter stopped and the hook removed, but the window kept showing the
  last guest frame. Four minutes later it still showed the 23:00 clock, also after the window was resized (the old
  frame stretched to the new size) and after a minimize and restore. The flip-model swap chain's last frame stays
  composited after the presenter lets go of it, so the GDI text painted underneath doesn't show. The user sees a
  frozen desktop, which is what L1 set out to avoid. Mode changes show the same for 40–60 ms (a black frame was caught
  once; harmless). Not covered by `DisplayOffTests`, which don't paint. **Fixed in code** (decision 0035): the
  presenter is kept and presents the message as an image through its normal path; only a session that never showed a
  frame paints it with GDI. **Live re-check (2026-10-31, below): pass.** The message replaced the frame 29 ms after
  the blank, stayed through drag-resize, maximize, minimize, 1:1 and full screen (redrawn sharp at each new size),
  Shift brought the picture back without a new presenter, and a disconnect while off showed the reason and Close.
- **L6: after the guest wakes its display, the surface's padding rows are shown again (the human's "strip at the
  bottom").** A capture across a wake (display channel, from blank to awake) has `SURFACE_CREATE 0 1600x960`, one
  DrawCopy of the whole 1600x960 surface, `MARK`, then DrawCopies of 0,0–1600x900 only, and no
  `DISPLAY_MONITORS_CONFIG` at all. The canvas forgets the head on every surface create (decision 0035 assumed that a
  config always follows), so the whole surface is shown. Its bottom 60 rows hold whatever the guest left in that
  memory (pieces of earlier frames: a dock icon, a terminal). A later fresh connection gets one head of 1600x960 (the
  surface size) from spice-server, so the viewer shows the same rows and sizes the window to 1600x960. A mode change
  does send a config, which is why the connect and mode-change checks pass. This isn't tearing within a frame: Upload
  and Present1 read the canvas under `Canvas.Sync`, and the strip is the extra rows. Pre-L2 builds show the whole
  surface all the time, so this isn't a regression from 0035 but a gap in it. One possible fix: keep the last head
  across a destroy and create while it still fits the new surface, as spice-gtk keeps its monitor list (not checked
  against remote-viewer). Scrubbed captures of both cases (the wake, and a fresh connect after a wake) are kept outside
  the repository for a replay fixture. **Fixed in code for a guest that blanks and wakes while connected, live
  re-check pending** (decision 0035): the head is kept across a new primary surface of the same size (not of another
  size: the win81 grow above sends no config either). Not fixed, and recorded as a limit: a session that starts with
  the guest already blank (the wake capture) has no head to keep, and the fresh connect after a wake gets a head
